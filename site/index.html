What is AQI?

AQI stands for Air Quality Index. It turns the amount of several pollutants in the air into one number between 0 and 500. A higher number means dirtier air. India’s Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) sorts the number into six bands, shown above, each with a name. The index uses a 24-hour average, so one bad hour does not move it much. It is set by whichever pollutant is worst, which in Pune is usually PM2.5, the fine dust explained further down.

    What are we breathing?

    Air pollution is a mix of several substances. These are the ones the AQI tracks.

    PM2.5
    Particulate matter smaller than 2.5 micrometres, about thirty times thinner than a human hair. These fine particles come from burning, vehicles and industry. They reach deep into the lungs.
    PM10
    Particulate matter smaller than 10 micrometres. This is coarser dust, from roads, construction and soil. It mostly irritates the nose and throat.
    NO₂ (nitrogen dioxide)
    A gas that comes mostly from traffic. It can inflame the airways.
    SO₂, CO, O₃, NH₃
    Sulphur dioxide, carbon monoxide, ozone and ammonia. They also count towards the AQI, but in Pune they decide it less often than dust and fine particles (see the chart below).

    Each pollutant gets its own score. The AQI you see is the highest of them. The pollutant with the highest score is called the dominant pollutant.

    What does the computer model say?

    This is not a station reading. It is an estimate from a computer model of the atmosphere, for the last 30 days and the next three. It can miss local events such as fireworks, so treat it as a rough guide.
